Session 17: Two-Stage Simulation Model

Summary#

Built a forward simulation to test whether contemporaneous economic fundamentals can reproduce the empirical Gini×cost gradient. The model has two stages: Stage 1 allocates athletes to sports via affordability × infrastructure × popularity; Stage 2 determines quality as a function of effective athletes, GDP, cost, and host bonus.

Key Design Decisions#

  • Affordability function: Maps income distribution (via Gini) to the fraction of households that can afford a sport’s annual cost. Tested multiple functional forms.
  • Infrastructure weighting: GDP × log(facility cost) determines national infrastructure capacity per sport.
  • Calibration target: Match empirical rich/poor GDP ratio (~2.57×) in simulated total points.
  • Monte Carlo: 20 simulation runs per configuration to account for stochastic allocation.

Results#

The simulation matches country-level totals reasonably well (r ≈ 0.90) but produces a Gini×cost gradient far smaller than the empirical value — roughly one-tenth to one-fifth of the observed coefficient in the individual sports, upper GDP half specification.

Principle#

The failure of the contemporaneous mechanism to reproduce the gradient motivated the search for a missing factor — leading to the persistence discovery in Sessions 18–19.

Claude: Analysis 8 is done. Here’s the headline:

The per-capita Gini result is a specification artefact. Six tests all point the same way:

  • Population elasticity is 0.615, significantly below 1 (t = −4.35, p < 0.001). Per-capita scoring forces α = 1, which is wrong.
  • With correct population scaling (α = 0.62), Gini weakens from p = 0.038 to p = 0.081 — crosses the 5% threshold.
  • The Gini×Pop interaction is completely dead (p = 0.924), so the confound operates through the denominator constraint, not through any substantive channel.
  • The effect exists only in small countries (p = 0.039) and vanishes in large ones (p = 0.270). Small equal countries (Nordics, Baltics, NZ) drive everything.
  • Total points with free population: Gini is null (p = 0.385). The sign literally flips from −0.68 to +4.34.

This means Geloso & Kufenko’s headline finding (equality → more medals per capita) is a methodological artefact of their dependent variable choice. All results, summary, and handover updated in the v0.96 folder.
