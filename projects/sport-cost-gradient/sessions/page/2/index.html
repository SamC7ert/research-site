Session 30: Simulation Deep Dive — Mechanisms and Concentration

Summary A systematic investigation of why the simulation reproduces only 40% of the empirical gradient. The empirical gradient (β = −0.68 for individual sports, upper GDP half) is concentrated in two countries: removing the United States and Russia reduces it by 82%. The simulation shows the same concentration pattern, meaning the 40% match is driven by the same outliers rather than a broadly operating mechanism. Experiments tested whether the affordability mechanism could be strengthened: different income distribution parameters, subsistence floors, quality curves. None closed the gap substantially. Session 25: Pipeline Divergence and v1.00 Data Preparation

Summary Reproducibility test 2 (an independent Claude session given the corrected v0.99.4 redacted paper with country-clustered SEs) produced results close but not matching: primary β = −0.698 (paper: −0.65), p = 0.032 (paper: 0.016), n = 1,882 (paper: 1,944). The gender decomposition hit sport name mapping bugs, and population elasticity required a log-log model form not obvious from the paper text. Rather than patching the paper with reproducibility numbers (an approach briefly attempted and then reverted), the decision was to trace the root cause. An exploration of the original pipeline (v0_99_game_level_pipeline.py) versus the reproducibility script revealed three divergence points in sample construction: (1) GDP median calculated from different sources (two-period average from predictors_master.json vs game-level GDP after positive-points filtering), (2) positive-points filter applied at different stages, (3) >= vs > median threshold producing 36 vs 35 upper-GDP countries.
