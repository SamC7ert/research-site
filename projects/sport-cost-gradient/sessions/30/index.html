Summary#

A systematic investigation of why the simulation reproduces only 40% of the empirical gradient. The empirical gradient (β = −0.68 for individual sports, upper GDP half) is concentrated in two countries: removing the United States and Russia reduces it by 82%. The simulation shows the same concentration pattern, meaning the 40% match is driven by the same outliers rather than a broadly operating mechanism.

Experiments tested whether the affordability mechanism could be strengthened: different income distribution parameters, subsistence floors, quality curves. None closed the gap substantially. The conclusion shifted from “simulation confirms the mechanism” to “simulation confirms the mechanism is insufficient — institutional channels dominate.”

5. Infrastructure function — Uses facilities cost, not equipment cost. Creates a GDP × facilities gradient, which feeds the GDP coefficient. It doesn’t interact with Gini at all.

6. Drain (non-Olympic sport siphoning) — Sport-specific and data-driven (cricket in India, rugby in NZ, etc.). Drain doesn’t correlate with Gini in a way that would boost the gradient. If anything, drain hits some rich countries (rugby nations) more than poor ones.

What’s structurally missing#

The model has exactly two channels through which Gini can interact with sport cost:

  • L1 affordability (income distribution × equipment cost)
  • L2 quality (GDP × non-equipment cost, with Gini entering only through GDP correlation)

Neither channel captures:

  • Institutional pipelines — club systems, federations, school sport programs that are structured by social class
  • Cultural capital — expensive sports carry social prestige that reinforces participation barriers beyond pure cost
  • Coaching/training quality — expensive sports have more professionalized coaching in rich countries
  • Multi-year compounding of selection — a 10-year development pipeline means affordability at age 8 determines medal chances at age 18, with dropout at every stage

These are all mechanisms where inequality interacts with sport cost, but they operate through institutions, not through the direct economic channel the model captures.

Bottom line#

Every free parameter either (a) is sport-agnostic (σ, α, df_t, religion, host), (b) is already at its constraint boundary (γ), or (c) overshoots other targets when pushed harder (β). The affordability function is the one mechanism that does the right thing, but its magnitude is structurally limited by deriving from income distributions rather than from institutional processes.
