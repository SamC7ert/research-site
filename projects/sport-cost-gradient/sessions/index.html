Session 44: NCAA Decomposition and Simpson's Paradox

Summary This session filled a placeholder in the paper by computing the US empirical-vs-simulated gap by sport, broken down by NCAA status. The result overturned the original framing: the data does not show a clean “US overperforms in cheap sports” pattern. Instead, the US overperforms the simulation in one sport (swimming, +366 points) and underperforms across most non-NCAA sports (-645 aggregate). The NCAA aggregate is almost exactly matched (-14). A Simpson’s Paradox was discovered: within each NCAA/non-NCAA group, the slope of US points vs equipment cost is positive or flat, but pooling across groups produces a negative slope. The between-group difference (NCAA sports are cheaper on average and the US dominates them) creates the appearance of a cost gradient that does not exist within groups. ...
