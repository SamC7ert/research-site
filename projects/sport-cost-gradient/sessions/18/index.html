Session 18: Affordability Curves and Persistence

Summary#

Tested five different affordability curve shapes (linear, moderate convex, strong convex, lognormal standard, lognormal fat-tail) to see whether the functional form of the income-to-participation mapping could close the gap between simulated and empirical Gini×cost gradients. None succeeded. Then introduced rolling persistence from previous Olympic Games, which closed the gap almost completely.

Affordability Curves Tested#

  1. Baseline (linear): Affordability = (1 − Gini) × income share above cost threshold
  2. Moderate convex (√): Square-root transformation of affordability
  3. Strong convex (^0.3): Power-law with exponent 0.3
  4. Lognormal standard: Full lognormal income distribution, standard σ
  5. Lognormal fat-tail (1.5σ): Fatter tails to amplify inequality effects

Key Finding#

All five curves produced simulated gradients that were 6–19% of the empirical value. The contemporaneous affordability mechanism is real but too weak to explain the data alone.

Persistence Introduction#

Added quality += γ × ln(1 + previous_points) per country-sport cell. Cultural persistence (fixed Sydney 2000 seed) and rolling persistence (previous game results) tested. Rolling persistence with γ ≈ 2.0 closes the gap.

Population → talent pool (the flattening curve)

  • Power law: N^α where α < 1. The standard. Simple, one parameter, well-established in the sports economics literature (Bernard & Busse 2004 use this). α ≈ 0.6 means 10× more people gives ~4× more talent.
